# Evaluation plan: Toward a Compositional Knowledge Architecture for a Shared Organizational Cortex

Andrew Miracle, Phyl Georgiou, Christine Okoth, Ese Monday

This is the evaluation specification for the paper. The registered text is git tag `registered-plan` (2026-10-20). Section numbers in the specification refer to the paper version current at that tag. The Dimension term, standing penalty, Domain scoping and HyDE escalation are scored in the paper from the retrieval record, on arms (i)–(viii). Arm (ix), facet-only, is recorded in `execution_record.yaml`. The bars, sample sizes, stopping rules and drop rules below are the registered specification (Miracle et al., 2026). Values the text calls initial settings ($k$, $\Delta$, $K$, the $\tau$ thresholds, and the thresholds of Section 5.4) are system parameters: they are tuned on validation splits and fixed before the held-out look. A component that meets its drop condition is removed in the form stated.

10. Evaluation Plan

Components. The plan evaluates exactly these nineteen components, each with baseline, metric, test set, threshold and drop condition in the subsection named; "shared" marks a test set from the shared protocol, which supplies annotation, leakage and stopping rules to all.

- Dimension term (10.1, shared).
- Standing penalty (10.1, shared).
- Domain scoping (10.1, shared).
- HyDE escalation (10.1, shared).
- Claims layer for artifacts (10.2).
- Deriver qualification (10.2).
- Signal-branch contradiction check (10.3).
- Claim-branch check and position filter (10.3).
- Propagation (10.3).
- Automatic acceptance (10.3).
- Supports verdicts (10.3).
- Entailment check (10.3).
- Compounding (10.4).
- Staleness (10.4).
- Cost ledger (10.4).
- Dimension discovery (10.5, shared).
- Projector qualification (10.5).
- Router (10.6).
- Domain classifier (10.6).

Not components. Every other named mechanism is covered inside a component's test or decides nothing:

- the cycle check and precondition migration: inside Propagation's global-relabelling comparison;
- lifting: inside the Signal-branch check; re-derivation: inside Compounding and Staleness; probe staleness: the Staleness metric;
- the decision model: inside the Router and Domain classifier;
- the Useful screen, the query-projection cost bound and projection staleness: inside the Dimension term;
- the permutation control and half-gain retention rule: attribution checks inside the Dimension term and discovery, layered on primaries that must already be significant;
- positive controls and outcome-neutral checks: pipeline checks that repair, not decide;
- the 10.1 system gains and the 10.2 decision rule: thesis tests (Section 10.7); the graph gain: reported only;
- natural-contradiction recall and the cross-Domain and after-Signal exclusions of Section 3.4: exploratory, since natural cases cannot be counted in advance to power a test;
- compute other than model calls: reported in machine-hours, not decided, except relabelling after Propagation's drop, which $M(m)$ carries;
- a smallest effect of practical interest: none beyond the design effect; the bar is a positive significant difference, reported with its interval.

Shared protocol. Experiments run on at least three pilot workspaces with distinct organizational subject matter. Query sets combine anonymized queries from usage logs with queries written by domain annotators; each workspace contributes at least 150 general queries, meaning queries not written for one component. Relevance judgments are graded (0 to 3) by two annotators per query over pooled top-20 results from every system under comparison (Zobel, 1998); inter-annotator agreement is reported as Cohen's $\kappa$ (Cohen, 1960) and queries with $\kappa < 0.4$, computed over the query's pooled items, are re-adjudicated. Each query set is split 40/60 into a validation split (for fitting $\beta_{D,d}$ and $\mu$, running the Useful screen, and setting thresholds) and a held-out test split that nothing is tuned on. Per-Domain fits require at least 30 validation queries scoped to the Domain (Section 6); Domains below that count are reported as unfit and excluded from the Dimension-aware arms for that workspace; workspaces are chosen so that at least their three largest Domains meet the minimum, and where the logs fall short annotators write validation queries for those Domains until each does. Held-out queries scoped only to unfit Domains still count in the pooled 10.1 primary, which they dilute, and the per-Domain breakdown reports it. Statistical significance uses a paired bootstrap over queries (Smucker, Allan, and Carterette, 2007), one-sided, unless a test names another procedure. Queries are deduplicated on normalized text before splitting; queries both annotators judge unanswerable from the workspace, and queries the organization withholds for personal data, are removed and counted; splits are by user session, so one session never spans two splits.

Annotation protocol. Annotators are pilot staff who know the subject matter, blind to arm, working from written instructions with these operative definitions. Relevance: 3, the item answers the query or is required to answer it; 2, it answers part of it; 1, it is on the query's topic but does not help answer it; 0, otherwise. A contradicting Signal is one sentence that, if true, makes the target Claim false under the target's own context, shares its named entities, and copies none of its other wording; a distractor is a sentence on the same entities consistent with the target. Domain labels are every Domain whose description the Signal falls under. Router labels are retrieve when stored items answer the request, reason when it asks for analysis or judgement without a document, and author when it asks for a document. Artifact requests written by annotators name an audience and a deliverable. Preference raters judge coverage (every part of the request is addressed), correctness (no statement is contradicted by the workspace's evidence), and coherence (statements agree with each other and are ordered for the audience), then state one overall preference. Quality control: before production each annotator labels 20 calibration items with adjudicated answers and must reach $\kappa = 0.6$ against them; $10\%$ of production items are hidden calibration items, and a batch whose annotator falls below $80\%$ agreement on them is relabelled; every item is labelled twice, agreement is reported as Cohen's $\kappa$ per task, and every disagreement goes to a third annotator.

Leakage. Three routes are closed. Claims derived for a request in a held-out session would put the test into the index, so Claims are built for evaluation only from requests in other sessions (for 10.1 and 10.2, the slow path run before any held-out look on every reason- or author-labelled request outside held-out sessions, the count reported), and any Claim whose derivation record names a held-out session is removed before scoring. The one exemption is the replay of Section 10.4, which measures that reuse: its reads are retrieve-labelled requests, which write nothing, so no read's own derivation enters the index, and the coefficient fit sees the replayed Claims' text but no held-out judgment. Synthetic queries from held-out Claims leak the Claim layer's vocabulary, so they may supplement but never replace judged queries, with results reported with and without them. Tuned components use only validation material, and no model judges an item it generated (the ALCE judge differs from the generator). Queries answerable only from Entries public on the web, and so possibly in pretraining data, are reported separately (exploratory).

Matched budgets. Every arm with tunable settings gets the same tuning budget on the validation split, 20 configurations sampled from its documented settings, and the same candidate depth, so no baseline runs at defaults against a tuned system (Dodge et al., 2019). Arms that call a reasoning or extraction model use the reference Deriver's model. Quality is compared at matched tuning budgets, not matched spend: the system's write spend, which no baseline incurs, is charged in the ledger of Section 10.4, where cost is compared at parity of quality under the cost-accounting rule below.

Success rules, power, and multiplicity. Every comparative test below states a design effect, the true difference the test is powered to detect, and sizes its sample so that a difference of that size is detected with 80% power. The 80% floor is the IR convention (Sakai, 2016) rather than the 90 to 95% of registered reports: a missed effect keeps a component out rather than harming anyone, and 90% would enlarge every judged sample by about 39%. The per-query standard deviation of $0.15$ for nDCG@10 differences is an assumed planning value with no published source; it budgets annotation and is replaced by the internal-pilot estimate below. The success rule is a positive, significant observed difference; the design effect is not a second bar, since requiring both would roughly halve power. The bar is not zero in practice: at the nominal one-sided $0.03$ of each look, significance requires an observed difference of at least $z_{0.97}/(z_{0.95} + z_{0.80}) \approx 0.76$ of the design effect at the planned size, and about $0.53$ of it at the cap of twice that size. A smaller observed gain can pass, so every gain is reported with its confidence interval. nDCG@10 is averaged because the decision concerns the expected per-query gain; values are bounded, the bootstrap assumes no normality, and the sign of per-query differences is reported as an ordinal check (exploratory). Each bet has one primary clause tested at one-sided $\alpha = 0.05$ or as the bet states; its other significance clauses are secondary and corrected as one family by Holm's step-down procedure (1979). A primary result that is positive but not significant triggers one prespecified extension of the sample by the same size, and the pooled sample is tested once more; as a two-look procedure, each look is tested at a nominal one-sided $0.03$ (Pocock, 1977), holding the overall type I error near $0.05$. Samples are sized at $\alpha = 0.05$, so power at the first look alone is about $73\%$, and the 80% floor is met over the two looks. Secondary clauses are tested once, on the sample at which the primary is decided. A primary that is still not significant after extension has failed, and a result at or below zero fails without extension.

Absolute bars and labels. A bar on a single proportion names a failure bound $p_0$ and a design value $p_1$; it is passed when an exact one-sided binomial test rejects $p \leq p_0$ at $\alpha = 0.05$, with the sample sized for 80% power at $p_1$, one look, and no extension. The primaries of Sections 10.2 and 10.4 are tested once, at one-sided $0.03$, with no extension. Ties never count as success: every clause needs a strictly positive significant difference or a rejected bound, except the non-inferiority clauses of Sections 10.1 and 10.5 and the fidelity guard of Section 10.2, whose margins are stated and which pass only when a confidence bound clears the margin. Primary and secondary clauses are confirmatory; per-workspace and per-Domain breakdowns, trends, latency percentiles and side-by-side generator comparisons are exploratory.

Variance re-estimation. For each primary comparison that cites this rule, the standard deviation of per-query differences is re-estimated once before the first look, from an internal pilot of the comparison's first 50 judged held-out queries pooled across workspaces (Wittes and Brittain, 1990); the stage size and the extension are recomputed from it. The interim step reports only the standard deviation, never the mean difference or any test. The recomputed size is never below the planned size, a restriction under which the type I error inflation of the naive t-test is often negligible (Wittes, Schabenberger, Zucker, Brittain, and Proschan, 1999), and never above twice it. Unlike adding topics until power is reached, which biases results toward significance (Webber, Moffat, and Zobel, 2008), this is one prespecified re-estimation, floored, capped and blind to the mean. Shortfall rule: when the cap, or the number of judged queries available, stops the pooled held-out count short of what the re-estimated standard deviation requires, the design effect is raised to what the available count can detect at the same power, and the raised figure is reported before scoring.

Cost accounting. Every model call in every arm is logged with model and tokens, and costs are reported as tokens per role and at the day's list prices. A request's cost, in any arm, is every model call it triggers, including the writes it causes. Costs are compared only between arms at quality parity or better; a less costly, worse arm is not a cost win.

10.1 Dimension-aware reranking

Bet: the Dimension term in Section 6 improves ranking over a strong retriever that has no access to conceptual positions, and the improvement is attributable to the geometry rather than to the standing penalty that ships with it.

Arms, all sharing the same retrieval stage over Signals and Claims (BM25 and dense retrieval fused by reciprocal rank fusion, reranked by a cross-encoder; fusion as in Cormack, Clarke, and Büttcher, 2009): (i) the hybrid baseline as stated; (ii) hybrid plus the standing penalty $\mu$ fit alone on the validation split, with no Dimension features; (iii) the full reranker, hybrid plus $\mu$ plus the Dimension term with $\beta_{D,d}$ and $\mu$ fit jointly; (iv) a permutation control, identical to (iii) except that each Claim's projection records are shuffled across Claims within the Domain before the coefficients are refit; (v) a Signals-only arm, the hybrid baseline with Claims removed from the index, the retrieval half of the alternative of Section 1; (vi) a global-retrieval arm, identical to (i) but unscoped by Domain; and (vii) a graph-augmented arm, entity-matched local retrieval over an entity graph built as in Edge et al. (2024) from the workspace's Entries with the reference Deriver's model, whose entities, relationships and source Signals join (i)'s candidates before the same cross-encoder; and (viii) the same graph-augmented arm built on (v), so that neither its index nor its graph holds Claims, the graph-augmented retrieval half of the alternative.

Primary metric: nDCG@10 (Järvelin and Kekäläinen, 2002) on the held-out split; secondary: Recall@50; MRR (Voorhees, 2000) is reported as exploratory only, since reciprocal rank is not interval-scaled and is not averaged into any decision. The Dimension gain is (iii) minus (ii); the standing gain is (ii) minus (i); the Claims-in-index gain is (i) minus (v); the system gain, full system over the alternative's retrieval, is (iii) minus (v) and (iii) minus (viii); the Domain-scoping gain is (i) minus (vi); the graph gain is (iii) minus (vii). Primary clause: the Dimension gain, design effect $0.03$ absolute nDCG@10, for which the pooled held-out general queries (about 270 across three workspaces) give at least 80% power at a planning standard deviation of $0.15$, re-estimated under the variance re-estimation rule (Section 10). Secondary clauses, five in the Holm family: the system gain is positive and significant against both (v) and (viii), each powered for $0.03$ (about 250 queries at the strictest Holm level, $0.01$, within the pooled 270), the clause passing only if both comparisons pass at its level, an intersection-union test that needs no further correction; in every workspace, the lower confidence bound on the Dimension gain at its Holm level lies above $-0.03$, a non-inferiority margin equal to the design effect because a workspace's sample cannot resolve a smaller one (with about 90 held-out queries per workspace, all three pass with about 80% probability when the true gain is $0.03$); the Domain-scoping gain is positive and significant, powered for $0.05$; the graph gain is positive and significant, powered for $0.03$, which the pooled queries cover at the strictest Holm level; and the standing gain, positive and significant, powered for $0.03$ on the same terms. The permutation control is the attribution test: shuffling removes all geometric information while $\mu$ is refit and survives, so the control is compared with (ii), and any gain of (iv) over (ii) is fitting noise; success requires that (iv) minus (ii) be at most half of (iii) minus (ii). Positive control, a check rather than an arm: on the validation split, an oracle feature equal to each Claim's judged relevance, fit as a Dimension term, must beat (ii) significantly, or the fitting and scoring pipeline is broken and is repaired before any held-out look. Cost: the query projection calls must add no more than $10\%$ to median fast-path cost per query for queries scoped to at most three Domains, which bounds the calls at $3K$; the bound is reported per scope size.

Drop conditions are separate for the two terms. The Dimension term is dropped if the Dimension gain fails under the shared success rule, or the permutation control retains more than half of it, or the cost bound is exceeded and the gain vanishes when query projection is disabled; dropping it also parks all Dimensions that were active only by the Useful screen. The standing penalty is kept if the standing gain is positive and significant and dropped otherwise, independently of the Dimension term. If the Domain-scoping gain is not positive and significant, scoping is removed from the fast path, and Domains remain only as the homes of Claims and Dimensions, with $w_q(d)$ taken over $c$'s home Domains. Unless the system gain is positive and significant against both (v) and (viii), the thesis fails on retrieval (Section 10.7). If the graph gain is not, graph-augmented retrieval matches the system and is reported as such. HyDE escalation is tested outside the Holm family, on the held-out queries where it fires: (iii) with escalation against (iii) without, by the same paired bootstrap at one-sided $0.05$, design effect $0.05$ nDCG@10, for which about 56 firing queries give 80% power. Unless at least that many fire and the difference is positive and significant, escalation is removed and the fast path makes no generation call.

10.2 The Claims layer

Bet: artifacts rendered from persisted Claims are better than artifacts produced without a persisted knowledge model, at comparable citation fidelity, and the improvement comes from the persisted model rather than from reasoning before rendering.

Baselines: (a) Signal-only generation, where the same retriever supplies proposition-level Signals directly to the same generator in one pass, which is the strongest form of the granularity argument from Chen et al. (2024); (b) a GraphRAG-style pipeline, where an entity graph with extracted entity claims, a step Edge et al. (2024) describe but did not evaluate, and community summaries is built over the workspace's Entries and supplied to the generator; and (c) per-request reasoning, the slow path of Section 7 run with persistence disabled: the same retriever, the reference Deriver's model, and the same renderer derive interpretations for the request, use them, and discard them, writing no Claims, no projections, and no standing. Baseline (c) is the artifact half of the alternative of Section 1, and it separates the persisted model from reasoning-before-rendering. Deriver qualification: the system as tested here uses the reference Deriver, which qualifies by the decision rule below; arm (d) runs the same test with a candidate Deriver, of any size, on the same requests against the same (b) and (c), and the candidate qualifies by the same rule, guard included (Section 7). Metrics: blinded pairwise preference on coverage, correctness, and coherence by two raters per pair, a third adjudicating disagreements, with pairs the third calls a tie excluded and reported; ALCE citation recall and precision (Gao, Yen, Yu, and Chen, 2023), computed with an NLI model and checked against human judgment on a $20\%$ subsample; a precondition check, the fraction of cited Claims with non-empty context whose preconditions appear in the artifact; and cost per artifact request for every arm, counted as the shared protocol counts it.

Test set: 85 artifact requests per pilot workspace, sampled uniformly from author-labelled requests in the logs outside the replay window of Section 10.4 and topped up by annotator-written requests where a workspace has too few (the split is reported), spanning at least 10 Domains per workspace, with audience specified (engineering or stakeholder), so that at least 225 decided pairs per baseline comparison remain after tie exclusion; if more than 30 are ties, further requests are drawn in a random order fixed in advance until 225 pairs are decided, before any preference is tested, and the number drawn is reported. The size follows from the strictest level at which any preference clause is tested, $0.05/3$ for the first Holm step, which also covers the primary's single test at $0.03$: a true $60\%$ preference rate against a $50\%$ null is then detected with $80\%$ power by an exact binomial test on about 225 pairs, which at that size means observing 129 or more wins. As a positive control, the generator with no evidence at all must lose to (a) significantly; if it does not, the raters are not discriminating, and the comparison is rerun with recalibrated raters before anything is scored.

Primary clause: preference against baseline (c), by exact binomial test, with $60\%$ as the design effect. Secondary: preference against (a) and (b). Guard, outside the Holm family: ALCE citation precision and recall non-inferior to the best baseline within $0.05$, by one-sided $95\%$ bootstrap bound over statements; a preference win that fails the guard is not a win. The precondition check must be $100\%$ (it is a renderer requirement, and any miss is a defect). Cost per artifact is not a success condition here; the persistence overhead is judged in Section 10.4's ledger. Decision rule: if the system is preferred significantly to (c) and to (b), the latter at its Holm level, and passes the guard, the persisted model contributes to artifact quality; otherwise, including when it beats (a) but not (c) or (b), the Claim layer adds nothing to artifacts that per-request reasoning or a graph pipeline without Claims lacks, the thesis fails on artifacts (Section 10.7), and artifact generation is dropped back to baseline (c), with Claims kept only if Section 10.1 keeps them for retrieval. A candidate Deriver that fails the rule does not fill the role. Drop condition: if the system is not significantly preferred to (a), granularity did the work, which is the Chen et al. outcome, and the Claim layer is dropped in favor of investment in Signal extraction; when the system beats (c) but not (a), this drop condition governs, whatever Section 10.1 finds. Nothing is tuned here: arms use the settings tuned on the 10.1 validation split, and all requests are held out.

10.3 Standing and dependency tracking

Bet: the contradiction check and propagation of Section 3.4 detect conflicts and retractions that matter, within bounded model calls and without regenerating Claims, and each branch of the check can be credited separately; and the entailment check of Section 3.1 decides the Signal/Claim boundary correctly.

Test set, two arms, both injected through the ordinary write path so the runtime performs classification, projection and the check, and both scored on conflicts_with edges by their branch tag. In the Signal arm, per workspace, 100 accepted Claims are targets; for each, an annotator writes one contradicting Signal and one same-topic distractor, and the 200 Signals are injected in random order over a simulated week, exercising the Signal branch (similarity with the floor $\tau_s$), lifting, and the Labeller. In the Claim arm, targets are 100 accepted Claims each with at least one active Dimension on which $r_d \geq \tau_r$, so the position filter can apply; for each, the annotator writes a contradicting Signal and a Claim justified by it alone that opposes the target on such a Dimension, plus a distractor Signal and Claim, each pair written as a unit, and the runtime projects each Claim in its batch before the check. The justifying Signal will usually also be caught by the Signal branch, as in production, so the Claim arm is scored only on Claim-branch edges. Pooled over three workspaces each arm has 300 targets.

Baselines, on copies of each workspace receiving the same injections: the exhaustive check, which runs the same classifier against every accepted Claim in the Domain with no candidate step and no floor, as a global recomputation would; the same candidate pairs labelled by the reference labeller instead of the Labeller; and, for the Claim branch, similarity-only candidates at the same $k$. The exhaustive check is also the positive control: if its own recall is below $0.7$, the classifier, not the candidate step, is failing, and the result is attributed to it. The Signal arm is also rerun on a copy at $k = 50$ with no floor, for the drop condition below. For propagation, a global relabelling recomputes every label from scratch after each batch; the network's labels must equal it exactly, with the consequence of the minimal-change check, and nodes visited per change are reported against the global count. The parameters $k$, $\tau_s$, $\tau_r$ and $\tau_\delta$ are set on 30 further targets per workspace per arm, injected the same way and disjoint from the scored ones.

Metrics, Signal arm: recall, the fraction of targets that acquire a Signal-branch edge to their contradicting Signal's lifted Claim; precision, the fraction of the arm's Signal-branch edges that join a lifted Claim to a Claim the annotator marked as contradicted by it; candidate recall, the fraction of injected contradictions whose target was among the $k$ candidates after the floor $\tau_s$; contradictions removed by the floor; and model calls per injected Signal. Claim arm: the same on Claim-branch edges; the fraction of checks in which the position filter applied; candidate precision with the filter versus similarity alone at the same $k$, over checks in which the filter applied; and model calls per injected Claim, including projection. Both arms: wall-clock from write to the contested transition. Retraction, both arms: a minimal-change check in which, after withdrawing the Entries behind 20 accepted Claims, the set of Claims that become retracted must equal the set whose every justification depended on those Entries, with no extra retractions, every Claim contested only by one of them must leave contested, and restoring the Entries must return exactly those Claims to proposed (Section 3.3). Automatic acceptance: annotators, blind to sample, judge whether the evidence supports 230 Claims the rule accepted in the replay of Section 10.4 (extended as that section states if it holds fewer) and, as baseline, 230 drawn at random from all Claims the slow path wrote, which is what accepting everything would accept; the samples overlap, which makes the comparison conservative. Entailment check: per workspace, 150 extracted Signals (is the proposition entailed by its span?) and 150 derivations (is the proposition entailed by no single antecedent?), drawn from ingest and the replay, labelled under the shared annotation QC and split 40/60 by Entry; the validation part sets any threshold, and each condition is scored once on the held-out part (about 270 pooled) by accuracy against the adjudicated label, with the false-pass rate reported. Baselines: the majority label and the reference labeller on the same items. Contradictions occurring naturally in the logs, found by annotators in a sample of 100 Claims per workspace, are scored for recall as an exploratory check on the injected sets' realism.

Primary clause: Signal-branch recall. Success, as absolute bars (Section 10): recall above $p_0 = 0.7$ with design value $0.8$ (119 targets give 80% power) and precision above $0.6$ with design value $0.7$ (143 edges) on each branch's own edges; candidate recall above $0.8$ with design value $0.9$ (82 targets) on each branch, with the floor removing none of the injected contradictions; the position filter raises candidate precision over similarity alone on the checks where it applied without lowering candidate recall below $0.9$; model calls per injected object at most $k + 1$ for the check, plus at most $K$ projection calls in the Claim arm; median wall-clock to the contested transition under $\Delta$ plus one minute; minimal-change check exact in both directions; and, because a supports verdict adds a justification that decides survival under retraction, the precision of supports verdicts, judged blind on 230 drawn at random from those the injected arms and the 10.4 replay wrote, above $0.7$ with design value $0.8$ and above a baseline of 230 random candidate pairs from the same checks (treating every candidate as support), by the same two-proportion test as below, and the same bar for the 230 automatically accepted Claims, whose precision must also exceed the baseline sample's by a one-sided two-proportion test at $0.05$, design effect $0.10$ (230 per sample give 80% power at $0.8$ against $0.7$); and entailment-check accuracy above $0.8$ with design value $0.9$ on each condition (83 items give 80% power), and above the majority label by McNemar's exact test.

Comparisons with the baselines are secondary, by McNemar's exact test over targets: if the exhaustive check's recall exceeds the system's significantly, the candidate step is losing contradictions the classifier would catch; if the reference labeller's precision exceeds the Labeller's significantly, the reference labeller fills the Labeller role, at the cost the ledger then carries.

Redesign conditions: Signal-branch candidate recall below $0.7$ means $k$ must rise, at linear cost, or $\tau_s$ must fall; if the position filter lowers Claim-branch candidate recall below $0.9$, the filter is removed and that branch falls back to similarity alone; precision below $0.5$ on either branch moves the Labeller role to another model that qualifies, or to a human queue; a minimal-change check or global-relabelling comparison that is not exact is a propagation defect, fixed and rerun before any other clause here is scored, at most twice. If it is still not exact after the second rerun, local propagation is dropped: every change triggers global relabelling of the workspace, which needs no model calls but visits every node, so the locality claim of Section 3.4 is withdrawn and the ledger reports the relabelling compute in $M(m)$. If automatic acceptance fails its bar or does not beat its baseline, the rule is disabled and every acceptance is made by a person, the ledger's second computation in Section 10.4. If supports-verdict precision fails its bar or does not beat its baseline, supports verdicts stop writing justifications and are logged for review only. If the entailment check fails either clause on a condition, the reference labeller takes that condition over if it passes the same bar on the held-out part, and is used anyway if it is significantly more accurate by McNemar's test; if both fail, the check is removed for that condition, Signals entering unchecked and flagged as such, or derivations losing the restatement check and every derived Claim staying proposed until a person accepts it. Drop condition: if Signal-branch recall at $k = 50$ with the floor removed is not significantly above $0.6$ (design value $0.7$; 143 targets give 80% power, and the arm has 300), automatic standing changes are disabled and standing becomes a purely human-maintained field. If Signal-branch recall fails the primary bar but passes this drop condition, the Signal branch runs at $k = 50$ with no floor, the passing configuration, at the ledger's cost.

10.4 Fast/slow compounding, staleness, and the cost ledger

Bet: accumulated slow-path work makes the fast path better over time and the structure does not go stale faster than it is maintained; the ledger measures what each role costs against the alternative's spend, and its break-even decides deployment, not the thesis.

Two replay series are run on each pilot workspace, both starting from a fresh copy containing only its Signals. The $m$ slow-path runs and the $N$ reads come from one window of the request log, in logged order and from held-out sessions only, so no replay read is in the validation split: requests labeled author or reason in Section 10.6 become the runs and those labeled retrieve the reads, so the overlap of writes and reads is the workspace's own. $N$ is at least 72 per workspace, 216 pooled, which gives 80% power for the design effect below at one-sided $0.03$ and a per-query standard deviation of $0.15$; because the log window fixes the read set, the primary here is tested once, at $0.03$, with no extension. Since the read set is judged only after the replay, its size is recomputed beforehand from the internal-pilot estimate of Section 10.1, and the realized standard deviation is reported, the detectable effect raised under the shortfall rule if it exceeds the value used. In the clean series only the slow-path runs change between checkpoints: the $N$ queries are replayed at $m = 0$ and after $m = 5$, $10$ and $20$, with the Signal corpus, query set and Dimension set frozen. Coefficients $\beta_{D,d}$ and $\mu$ are fit once, on the validation split against the final checkpoint's Claims, and applied at every checkpoint (at $m = 0$ the Dimension term is inert). Claims first entering any arm's top 20 at a checkpoint get a post-replay pooling and judging pass before scoring. This series measures compounding and read cost. Positive control, a deliberate leak used only as a check: on a copy at $m = 20$, the reads' own requests are run once as reason, and the fast path must then beat $m = 0$ significantly, or the pipeline is repaired before scoring. The injected series performs the same runs and, at each checkpoint, injects a contradiction set against the accepted Claims then present; it measures staleness and per-Signal ingest cost, kept apart so injected noise cannot pass for compounding.

Contradiction set construction. At checkpoint $m$, annotators select up to 100 accepted Claims from those that exist, and for each write one contradicting Signal, following the Section 10.3 Signal-arm procedure. With fewer than 20 accepted Claims at a checkpoint, staleness there is reported as not measurable rather than as zero.

Quality and staleness metrics at each $m$ on the clean series: fast-path nDCG@10; HyDE escalation rate; and p50 and p95 query latency. On the injected series: the staleness rate, defined as the fraction of top-10 results that are Claims whose standing is accepted or proposed while an injected Signal contradicting them has been present for longer than the batch interval $\Delta$. A contested Claim shown with its conflict is not counted. Retracted or superseded Claims in any top 10 must number zero (Section 3.3); any is a retrieval defect, not staleness. Primary clause: the gain in nDCG@10 from $m = 0$ to $m = 20$ on the clean series, positive and significant by paired bootstrap over the $N$ queries, powered for a design effect of $0.03$. Secondary: no checkpoint significantly below the checkpoint before it; the escalation rate falls; p95 latency does not rise. Because that rate is diluted when reads rarely reach the targeted Claims, the tested bar uses probes: after $\Delta$ has elapsed, each injected target's own proposition is issued as a query, and probe staleness is the fraction of probes whose top 10 contains the target at accepted or proposed standing. Success on the injected series: probe staleness below $0.10$, design value $0.05$, by exact one-sided binomial test pooled at the final checkpoint (about 180 probes give 80% power; up to 300 are available), with the all-results rate reported at every checkpoint.

Arms for the injected series: the full system as specified, and a Graphiti-style arm (Rasmussen et al., 2025) ingesting the same Signals incrementally into a temporal fact graph that invalidates temporally overlapping contradicted facts; its probe staleness counts probes whose top 10 holds a contradicted fact not yet invalidated. Per-Signal ingest cost is measured for both: the system's classifier and lifting calls, written $c_{\text{ing}}$, and the arm's extraction and invalidation calls. If the full system's probe staleness is not significantly below this arm's, by a one-sided two-proportion test at $0.05$ on the pooled final-checkpoint probes (about 110 per arm give 80% power at $0.05$ against $0.15$), at a per-Signal ingest cost no lower than the arm's, the network is not earning its complexity on this axis, and, binding, the Signal branch of the check and lifting are removed, so Signals act on Claims only through supersession and withdrawal.

Read arms on the clean series. The fast path is compared with two read arms holding no Claims: arm (v) of Section 10.1, and the same retrieval followed by the reference Deriver's model, the most the alternative would spend on a read. Quality is compared on the same $N$ judged queries at the reasoning arm's evidence depth: with $|C_q|$ the Signals its answer cites, every arm's list is truncated to its top $|C_q|$ and scored by nDCG and precision there. A baseline arm reaches parity when the system is not significantly better than it, deliberately absence of significance rather than an equivalence margin, the stricter rule against the system, since the least costly arm the system cannot beat sets the read baseline. That arm's per-query cost is $r_{\text{base}}$; if none reaches parity, it is the reasoning arm's cost.

Cost ledger. Every arm serves the same workload: $m$ slow-path requests, $N$ reads, and the Signals ingested while a workspace produces $m$ requests. The system is charged five lines. $W(m)$ is the cost of the $m$ slow-path runs on the clean series. $M(m)$ is automated maintenance over the same period: re-derivation after relabelling, re-projection and version backfill, Stable re-checks, discovery and validation, and coefficient refits, logged on the injected series, prorated per evaluation cycle, and with its relabelling share rescaled from the injected contradiction rate to the rate of withdrawals and supersessions in the replay window. $m \, \sigma \, c_{\text{ing}}$ is ingest checking, where $\sigma$, Signals ingested per slow-path request, comes from the same pilot logs as $\bar{N}$, the read queries per slow-path request, and $c_{\text{ing}}$, the classifier and lifting cost per Signal, from the injected series; the alternative has no such line, since it only embeds and indexes Signals, as the system also does. $N \cdot r_{\text{fast}}$ is reads at the fast path's mean per-query cost at $m = 20$, including query projection, the decision-model call of Section 7 at a per-call cost $c_{\text{dec}}$, and any HyDE escalation. $H(m) = L \sum_a n_a(m)\, t_a$ is human curation, over the actions the design requires of people: acceptance and re-acceptance after retraction, conflict resolution, review of Dimension replacements, pins and retirements, and Domain creation and revision. The counts $n_a$ are the actions the replay leaves for people (Claims awaiting acceptance, recorded conflicts, queued Dimension reviews) plus the Domain creations and revisions in the log window, the initial authoring of the workspace's Domains prorated over it, and the description revisions and reclassifications of Section 10.6; the minutes $t_a$ are the median of 30 timed performances of each action by the pilot's reviewers, and $L$ is the loaded hourly cost of the reviewing role in the pilot organization, stated with the ledger. Domain creation, with its re-scoring of Signals, is also reported as its own line and is charged to the system although the alternative scopes by the same Domains, so that no curation cost is left out, and the ledger is computed twice, with automatic acceptance on and with every acceptance made by a person. It is also computed with each replayed request charged the cost of the path the Router, as deployed, sends it to, so that escalations and misroutes are paid for; the success condition must hold under both routings. For the alternative, each slow-path request is served by baseline (c) of Section 10.2 at its measured cost $g_{\text{base}}$ and each read at $r_{\text{base}}$, so its artifact and read baselines are one system, with total $m \cdot g_{\text{base}} + N \cdot r_{\text{base}}$. The break-even read count is

$$N^{*} = \frac{W(m) + M(m) + H(m) + m \, \sigma \, c_{\text{ing}} - m \cdot g_{\text{base}}}{r_{\text{base}} - r_{\text{fast}}}$$

the reads over which the system's extra write, maintenance, curation and ingest cost is repaid; $N^{*}/m$ is the break-even read/write ratio. A uniform change in model prices moves $N^{*}/m$ only through $H$, which does not scale with them, so as prices fall the human term dominates. The ratio of the Deriver's per-call price to the reader and Labeller models' moves $W$, $g_{\text{base}}$ and, with the reasoning arm at parity, $r_{\text{base}}$, but not $H$, ingest or $r_{\text{fast}}$: with the reasoning arm at parity a higher ratio lowers $N^{*}/m$ and a lower one can remove break-even, as the illustration shows; with plain retrieval at parity $N^{*}/m$ rises with the ratio. $N^{*}/m$ rises linearly in $\sigma$, with slope $c_{\text{ing}}/(r_{\text{base}} - r_{\text{fast}})$. The ledger reports $N^{*}/m$ at the day's list prices and on a grid of $0.25$, $0.5$, $1$, $2$ and $4$ times that price ratio and $0.5$, $1$ and $2$ times $L$, each against $\bar{N}$, and it reports the projection and ingest lines separately, which answers the question Section 5.5 leaves open. An illustration with supposed prices, not estimates (a reader or Labeller call \$0.0005, a Deriver call \$0.025, reviewer \$60 an hour): suppose each slow-path request costs 20 Deriver calls and 8 projections in $W$ (\$0.504), \$0.05 in $M$, 30 seconds of review in $H$ (\$0.50), and 30 ingested Signals at $k + 1 = 21$ Labeller calls each (\$0.315), against $g_{\text{base}}$ of 15 Deriver-priced calls (\$0.375); and suppose a fast-path read costs the equivalent of 30 reader calls, mostly cross-encoder scoring with query projection within the $10\%$ bound of Section 10.1, plus $c_{\text{dec}}$, against one Deriver-priced call and two reader calls (\$0.026) for the reasoning arm. Then $N^{*}/m = 0.994/(0.011 - c_{\text{dec}})$: about 90 reads per slow-path request when $c_{\text{dec}}$ is negligible, and no break-even once $c_{\text{dec}}$ reaches \$0.011. Halving the Deriver's price removes the break-even, since the reasoning read falls to \$0.0135, below the fast path's \$0.015; doubling it lowers $N^{*}/m$ to about 31, or 32 if $M$, being model spend, doubles too; doubling the labor rate raises it to about 136; and if plain retrieval reaches parity there is no break-even at any of these prices.

The observed ratio $\bar{N}$, read queries per slow-path request, is measured from pilot logs, not assumed. The success condition is $N^{*} \leq \bar{N} \cdot m$ at $m = 20$: the structure pays for itself within the reads a workspace actually performs. It is tested as a bound: the one-sided $95\%$ bootstrap upper bound on $N^{*}$, resampling slow-path requests, reads and timed actions together, must lie at or below $\bar{N} \cdot m$, and a resample whose denominator is at or below zero counts as no break-even. "$N^{*}$ above $\bar{N} \cdot m$" below and in Section 10.7 means that this bound is above it. The test is sized before the replay by simulation from internal-pilot costs and the pilot-log $\bar{N}$, for 80% power at a minimum detectable margin of a true $N^{*}$ at half of $\bar{N} \cdot m$: the reads and the slow-path requests costed are increased up to three times the planned counts, the extra reads and runs being costed only, never judged, so the quality samples and their cap are unchanged, and the runs drawn from the same log window also fill the acceptance samples of Section 10.3. Sizes are reported before scoring, and a shortfall at the cap moves the margin under the shortfall rule. The condition $r_{\text{fast}} < r_{\text{base}}$ is necessary for any break-even and is reported as a sanity check, not as a success.

Drop conditions: no significant gain by $m = 20$ on the clean series means slow-path outputs are not being reused; probe staleness failing its bar means the structure decays faster than propagation maintains it, the weaker failure of Section 1; and $N^{*}$ above $\bar{N} \cdot m$ under either routing, or no break-even at all, means the structure does not repay its cost at the observed ratio, a deployment finding rather than a test of the thesis. Any of these returns the system to the alternative.

10.5 Dimension discovery and projection

Bet: the discovery pipeline of Section 5.4 produces Dimensions that rank as well as Dimensions people author and that people recognize as meaningful, and a model trained on the reference projections can qualify as Projector.

Baselines: human-authored Dimensions, and for projection the reference projector itself. Before any held-out query is seen, one domain annotator per Domain authors up to $K$ Dimensions under the contract of Section 5.1 from the Domain description and a sample of its Claims, in a session of at most two hours timed for the ledger of Section 10.4; these pass the same validation and are projected by the same projector. Metrics and test set: the Dimension gain of Section 10.1 on its held-out queries, once with the discovered active sets (arm (iii)) and once with the human-authored ones (arm (iii-h), coefficients refit); meaningfulness, where two raters blind to each promoted Dimension's source answer "would you use this axis to compare these Claims?" on ten projected Claim pairs, a Dimension counting as meaningful when both say yes on a majority, $\kappa$ reported; the two candidate generators' promotion and meaningfulness rates side by side (exploratory); and agreement, $r_s$, between a candidate Projector trained on the reference bootstrap and the reference projector, on 30 to 200 held-out Claims per Dimension, with the ratio of per-projection costs.

Two outcome-neutral checks come first: projecting each Dimension's own prototypes must return their assigned values, or the projection pipeline is broken; and the human-authored set must itself help, (iii-h) minus (ii) positive and significant, or the comparison has no assay sensitivity, and discovery stands or falls with the Dimension term in Section 10.1. Primary clause: discovered Dimensions are non-inferior to human-authored ones in ranking, that is, the one-sided $95\%$ paired-bootstrap lower bound on (iii) minus (iii-h) lies above $-0.03$ nDCG@10, and, since that margin equals the whole 10.1 design effect, the discovered sets must also retain at least half the human-authored gain, (iii) minus (ii) at least half of (iii-h) minus (ii), a point comparison like the permutation control's. The claim is parity with human authoring at lower human cost; a zero difference passes only if the bound clears the margin, and the pooled 10.1 queries give 80% power for a $0.03$ margin at a true difference of zero. Secondary, as absolute bars: meaningful promoted Dimensions above $0.5$ with design value $0.75$, for which 23 Dimensions give 80% power, with discovery run for up to three cycles to reach 40 pooled and fewer than 23 after three cycles counted as a failure; and the share of active Dimensions on which the candidate Projector meets the Measurable threshold, $r_s \geq 0.7$ against the reference projector, above $0.6$ with design value $0.85$ (21 Dimensions), at a per-projection cost no more than one tenth of the reference projector's. Drop conditions: if the primary or the meaningfulness bar fails, automatic discovery is removed and Dimensions are human-authored per Domain, with validation and projection unchanged; if the projector bar fails, the reference projector keeps the Projector role and $c_\pi$ becomes the binding constraint on the number of active Dimensions, lowering $K$ and raising $W(m)$ in the Section 10.4 ledger.

10.6 Router and Domain classifier

Bet: the two routing decisions the fast path depends on are accurate enough that the failures of Sections 10.1 through 10.4 can be attributed to the components under test rather than to misrouting.

Router. Test set: 300 held-out logged requests per workspace, 900 pooled, labeled retrieve, reason, or author by two annotators, $\kappa$ reported; a further 200 per workspace, disjoint by session, are the validation part that sets the Router's confidence threshold and fits the embedding baseline, and the labels also route the replay of Section 10.4. Metrics: accuracy, and the two misroute rates separately, since a slower path wastes compute and a faster one gives a shallower answer. Baselines: the majority label, and the embedding baseline, a multinomial logistic regression over request embeddings fit on the validation part under the matched tuning budget; a reference model prompted with the label definitions is reported (exploratory), since the role goes to whichever model meets the bars, not to the one that matches it. Outcome-neutral checks: the embedding baseline must beat the majority label, or the labels are noise; and expected calibration error over ten bins must be below $0.05$ for the confidence threshold to be used, the top label being taken otherwise. Primary clause, as an absolute bar: accuracy above $0.85$ with design value $0.9$ (about 280 requests give 80% power; the pooled set has 900). Secondary: accuracy above the embedding baseline by McNemar's exact test, design effect $0.05$ (at a discordance rate of $0.15$ about 370 requests give 80% power, and about 470 at the stricter Holm level); and the shallow-misroute rate below $0.10$ with design value $0.05$ (about 180 requests). Drop condition, applied before any downstream result is interpreted: if the primary or either secondary fails, the Router is removed; the embedding baseline replaces it if it passes the primary bar, and otherwise the user chooses retrieve, reason or author.

Domain classifier. Test set: 800 Signals per workspace, labelled by two annotators, $\kappa$ reported per Domain, and split 40/60 by Entry like the query sets; thresholds $\theta_D$, the per-Domain check below and any description revision use the validation part, and the clauses are scored once on the held-out part, stratified so each Domain has at least 40 Signals there. Baselines: the majority Domain; nearest-description similarity with one global threshold, which must itself beat the majority Domain as an outcome-neutral check; and one-vs-rest logistic regression over Signal embeddings fit on the validation part, the stronger of the two embedding baselines on that part being the comparison below; a prompted reference model is reported (exploratory); calibration of the returned probabilities is tested as for the Router before $\theta_D$ is set from them. Metrics: per-Domain precision and recall, and macro-F1. Primary clause: macro-F1 above $0.80$ by one-sided $95\%$ bootstrap bound, resampling Entries so that Signals from one Entry move together, design value $0.85$; with at least 40 held-out Signals in each of 30 or more pooled Domains, a binomial approximation at the design value puts the standard error of macro-F1 near $0.01$, so power is far above 80%. Secondary: macro-F1 above the stronger embedding baseline by the same paired bootstrap, design effect $0.04$, which with independent errors has a standard error near $0.014$ and about 85% power; the realized standard error is reported, and the detectable effect raised under the shortfall rule if it is larger. Per-Domain recall of at least $0.75$ matters, since a missed Signal is invisible to queries scoped to that Domain, but per-Domain samples are too small to test it, so on the validation part a Domain below it has its description revised and Signals reclassified before Section 10.1 runs (exploratory). Drop condition: if the primary or secondary fails, the classifier is removed from the fast path, Domain scope stops being a hard filter, and the workspace runs as arm (vi) of Section 10.1.

10.7 What falsifies the thesis

The thesis fails if either comparison with the alternative fails: if the full system's gain over the Signals-only hybrid arm or the graph-augmented arm without Claims is not positive and significant in Section 10.1, or if it is not significantly preferred to per-request reasoning without persistence and to the GraphRAG-style pipeline in Section 10.2, the persisted structure is not improving that part of what users see. It fails in the weaker sense if probe staleness fails its bar in Section 10.4. A role is filled only by a model that passed its bar; if no model qualifies, the component that needs the role is dropped in the form its subsection states. Section 10.4's break-even is reported and decides deployment, not the thesis. A component that meets its own drop condition is removed, and the thesis is judged on the rest; a framework that fails remains research, not architecture.
