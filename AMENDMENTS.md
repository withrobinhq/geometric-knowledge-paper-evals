# Amendments

## Registered plan (tag registered-plan), 2026-10-20

The plan is consolidated into one general document under the paper's new title, with all four authors. Its tests are organized around writer and reader models: each model takes a role only after passing that role's qualification test, and the earlier untested premise about which model should derive becomes the Deriver's qualification test. The cost ledger and its break-even condition are kept as deployment rules.

## Version 5 (tag plan-v5), 2026-10-20

The plan now states a closed list of the components it evaluates, each with its baseline, metric, test set, threshold and drop condition, and lists every other mechanism of the design with the reason it is not a separate component. Supports verdicts gain a baseline.

Each item is what changed in Section 10, before → after, and why.

1. Registration. Before: version 4, tag plan-v4. After: "version 5 of a registration whose amendments are public", and the reference reads "Version 5 … tag plan-v5". Why: this is the fifth registered version of the plan.
2. Closed list of components. Before: components were described subsection by subsection, and the plan did not say which mechanisms counted as components. After: a list at the top of Section 10 names exactly eighteen components. Each has its baseline, metric, test set, threshold and drop condition in the subsection named. "Shared" marks a test set supplied by the shared protocol, which also supplies annotation, leakage and stopping rules to all. Why: the plan now states its own scope.
3. Mechanisms that are not components. Before: no such statement. After: a list says where every other named mechanism is decided:
   - the cycle check and precondition migration: inside the propagation test;
   - lifting, re-derivation and probe staleness: inside the contradiction-check, compounding and staleness tests;
   - the decision model: inside the Router and Domain classifier tests;
   - the Useful screen, the query-projection cost bound and projection staleness: inside the Dimension-term test;
   - the permutation and half-gain rules: attribution checks layered on primaries that must already be significant;
   - positive controls and outcome-neutral checks: pipeline checks;
   - system gains and the artifact decision rule: thesis tests (10.7);
   - the graph gain and the small-tier deriver: reported only;
   - natural-contradiction recall and the cross-Domain and after-Signal exclusions: exploratory, since natural cases cannot be counted in advance;
   - compute other than model calls: reported in machine-hours, except relabelling after the propagation drop, which M(m) carries;
   - a smallest effect of practical interest: none beyond the design effect.
   Why: no named mechanism is left without a stated status.
4. Supports verdicts (10.3). Before: 150 sampled justifications of unstated source, judged against an absolute bar only. After:
   - Sample: 230 supports verdicts drawn at random from those the injected arms and the 10.4 replay wrote, judged blind.
   - Baseline: 230 random candidate pairs from the same checks, which is what treating every candidate as support would add.
   - Bar: precision above 0.7 (design value 0.8), and above the baseline by the same one-sided two-proportion test as automatic acceptance (design effect 0.10; 230 per sample give 80% power).
   - Drop: supports verdicts stop writing justifications if either clause fails.
   Why: the component now has something to beat and a stated sample source. The sample size was raised to power the comparison.
5. Signal-branch recall between the drop bound and the primary bar (10.3). Before: recall that failed the primary bar but passed the drop condition changed nothing. After: "the Signal branch runs at k = 50 with no floor, the passing configuration, at the ledger's cost". Why: every failing outcome now has a consequence.
6. Index for 10.1 and 10.2 (Leakage). Before: Claims came "only from requests in other sessions", with no count or source. After: "the slow path run before any held-out look on every reason- or author-labelled request outside held-out sessions, the count reported". Why: the index behind the Claims-in-index and system gains is now specified.
7. Agreement triggers (shared protocol). Before: queries and items "with κ < 0.4" were re-adjudicated, which is undefined for a single item. After: a query's κ is "computed over the query's pooled items", and "every disagreement goes to a third annotator". Why: the triggers can be executed, and adjudication is broader than before.
8. Planning standard deviation. Before: 0.15 was stated without a source. After: "an assumed planning value with no published source", still replaced by the internal-pilot estimate before any final sample size is set. Why: states its status.
9. N̄ defined at first use (10.4). Before: N̄ was used before its definition. After: "the same pilot logs as N̄, the read queries per slow-path request". Why: reading order.
10. Wording: the introduction's list of fields per subsection was removed, since the component list now carries it. No threshold, sample, stopping rule or drop condition changed.

## Version 4 (tag plan-v4), 2026-10-16

The thesis gains two ways to fail (a graph-augmented retrieval arm built without persisted Claims, and the graph pipeline baseline entering the artifact decision rule), and the propagation check gains a binding drop condition.

Each item is what changed in Section 10, before → after, and why.

1. Registration. Before: version 3, tag plan-v3. After: "version 4 of a registration whose amendments are public", and the reference reads "Version 4 … tag plan-v4". Why: this is the fourth registered version of the plan.
2. New retrieval arm (10.1). Before: the only graph-augmented arm, (vii), joined its graph candidates to an index that holds the system's Claims. After: adds "(viii) the same graph-augmented arm built on (v), so that neither its index nor its graph holds Claims, the graph-augmented retrieval half of the alternative". Why: the alternative to persistence includes graph-augmented retrieval, so it needs an arm without Claims.
3. System gain (10.1). Before: the system gain was (iii) minus (v) only. After: it is "(iii) minus (v) and (iii) minus (viii)". The clause passes only if the system is positive and significant against both, each powered for 0.03 at the clause's Holm level, an intersection-union test that needs no further correction. Unless both pass, the quality half fails on retrieval. The Holm family still has five clauses. Why: adds a way for the thesis to fail.
4. Artifact decision rule (10.2). Before: the persisted model counted as contributing if the system was preferred significantly to (c) and passed the guard. After: it must be preferred significantly to (c) and to the GraphRAG-style pipeline (b), the latter at its Holm level, and pass the guard. Otherwise the quality half fails on artifacts and generation falls back to (c). Why: a graph pipeline without Claims is part of the alternative; adds a way for the thesis to fail.
5. What falsifies the thesis (10.7). Before: the quality half failed against the Signals-only arm or per-request reasoning. After: it also fails against "the graph-augmented arm without Claims" and "the GraphRAG-style pipeline". Why: consistent with items 3 and 4.
6. Propagation drop condition (10.3). Before: a minimal-change check or global-relabelling comparison that was not exact was "fixed and rerun", with no limit and no drop. After: it is fixed and rerun "at most twice". If it is still not exact after the second rerun, local propagation is dropped: every change triggers global relabelling of the workspace, which needs no model calls but visits every node, so the locality claim of Section 3.4 is withdrawn and the ledger reports the relabelling compute in M(m). Why: the propagation mechanism now has a binding consequence if it fails.

## Version 3 (tag plan-v3), 2026-10-15

The entailment check that gates every write becomes a confirmatory component with its own baseline, bar and binding drop rule; every other change adds a test, sample or consequence, corrects wording, or clarifies.

Each item is what changed in Section 10, before → after, and why.

1. Registration. Before: version 2 of the registration (tag plan-v2). After: "registered publicly before any data existed, as version 3 of a registration whose amendments are public", tag plan-v3 in the reference. Why: this version of the plan is the third registered one.
2. Graph-augmented arm (10.1, arm vii). Before: "local search in the manner of Edge et al. (2024)". After: "entity-matched local retrieval over an entity graph built as in Edge et al. (2024)". Why: the cited work builds the graph but does not describe local search. The arm's method is unchanged.
3. Entailment check (10.3) becomes a confirmatory component. Before: annotators labelled 150 extracted Signals and 150 derivations, and the error rate was reported as exploratory. After:
   - Test set: per workspace, 150 extracted Signals (is the proposition entailed by its span?) and 150 derivations (is it entailed by no single antecedent?), drawn from ingest and the replay, labelled under the shared annotation quality control and split 40/60 by Entry. The validation part sets any threshold, and each condition is scored once on the held-out part (about 270 pooled).
   - Metric: accuracy against the adjudicated label, with the false-pass rate reported.
   - Baselines: the majority label, and the frontier labeller on the same items.
   - Bar: accuracy above 0.8 with design value 0.9 on each condition (83 items give 80% power), and above the majority label by McNemar's exact test.
   - Binding drop: if either clause fails on a condition, the frontier labeller takes that condition over if it passes the same bar, and is used anyway if it is significantly more accurate. If both fail, the check is removed for that condition: Signals enter unchecked and flagged as such, or derivations lose the restatement check and every derived Claim stays proposed until a person accepts it.
   - The bet sentence of 10.3 now names the check.
   Why: the check gates every write, so it now has a baseline, a metric, a test set, a bar and a replacement.
4. Supports verdicts (10.3). Before: the precision bar had no consequence. After: if it fails, "supports verdicts stop writing justifications and are logged for review only". Why: supports verdicts decide survival under retraction.
5. Global relabelling (10.3). Before: propagation was checked only against the minimal-change oracle. After: a global relabelling recomputes every label from scratch after each batch. The network's labels must equal it exactly, with the same consequence as the minimal-change check, and nodes visited per change are reported against the global count. Why: compares local relabelling with the global recomputation it replaces.
6. Drop-condition run (10.3). Before: the drop condition used Signal-branch recall "at k = 50 with the floor removed", but no such run was specified. After: "The Signal arm is also rerun on a copy at k = 50 with no floor, for the drop condition below." Why: the drop condition now has the run it reads.
7. Validation for 10.3 parameters. Before: k, τ_s, τ_r and τ_δ had no stated validation set. After: they are set on 30 further targets per workspace per arm, injected the same way and disjoint from the scored ones. Why: parameters are tuned on validation material only, as the shared protocol requires.
8. 10.2 tuning. Before: no validation split was stated. After: "Nothing is tuned here: arms use the settings tuned on the 10.1 validation split, and all requests are held out." Why: states where tuning happens.
9. 10.2 drop and decision rule. Before: when the system beats (c) but not (a), the drop condition (Claim layer dropped) and the decision rule (Claims kept if 10.1 keeps them) could both apply. After: "this drop condition governs, whatever Section 10.1 finds". Why: resolves the conflict in the stricter direction.
10. 10.4 stopping rule and sample. Before: N was at least 60 per workspace (180 pooled), powered at 0.05, and whether the shared extension applied was unstated. After: N is at least 72 per workspace (216 pooled), giving 80% power at one-sided 0.03. Because the log window fixes the read set, the primary is tested once, at 0.03, with no extension. The shared single-test sentence now names 10.2 and 10.4. Why: removes the ambiguity in the stricter direction and raises the sample to keep power.
11. Shared protocol, test levels. Before: "Each bet has one primary clause tested at one-sided α = 0.05". After: "… at one-sided α = 0.05 or as the bet states". Added: "Secondary clauses are tested once, on the sample at which the primary is decided." Why: consistency with items 10 and the 10.2 single test, and a fixed look for secondaries.
12. Per-Domain validation queries. Before: "workspaces are chosen so that at least their three largest Domains meet the minimum". After: the same, and "where the logs fall short annotators write validation queries for those Domains until each does". Held-out queries scoped only to unfit Domains still count in the pooled 10.1 primary, which they dilute, and the per-Domain breakdown reports it. "General queries" is defined as queries not written for one component. Why: guarantees the fits the plan needs and states the dilution.
13. Routing in the ledger (10.4, 10.7). Before: replayed requests were routed by annotator labels, so Router escalations and misroutes cost nothing. After: the ledger is also computed with each request charged the cost of the path the deployed Router sends it to. The success condition must hold under both routings, and "N* above N̄·m" now reads "under either routing" in 10.4 and 10.7. Why: routing costs are paid in deployment.
14. Human curation counts (10.4). Before: n_a counted the actions the replay leaves for people plus Domain creations and revisions in the log window. After: it also counts "the initial authoring of the workspace's Domains prorated over it, and the description revisions and reclassifications of Section 10.6". Why: no curation cost is left out.
15. Per-workspace margin (10.1). Before: a margin equal to the design effect, with no reason given. After: the same margin, "because a workspace's sample cannot resolve a smaller one". Why: the margin is unchanged, and the reason is now stated.
16. Paragraphing (10.3). The success, comparison and redesign blocks are now separate paragraphs. Paragraphing only.
17. Wording tightened in the shared protocol (power justification, standard-deviation note, averaging, variance re-estimation, leakage, cost accounting), 10.1 (permutation control, HyDE), 10.2 (cost sentence), 10.4 (series, read arms, sensitivity, illustration) and 10.5 (non-inferiority note). No threshold, sample, stopping rule or drop condition changed.

## Version 2 (tag plan-v2), 2026-10-15

Every change adds a baseline, test, margin or sample, corrects arithmetic, or clarifies wording. One secondary metric (MRR) moved to exploratory. In the paper, the introduction to Section 10 now carries one sentence pointing to this registration.

Each item is what changed in Section 10, before → after, and why.

1. Registration. Before: the bars, bounds, sample sizes, stopping rules and drop rules "are fixed by this paper". After: they "were registered publicly before any data existed, do not change once held-out data has been scored, and any deviation is reported against that registration (Miracle, 2026)". Why: the plan is now fixed outside the paper.
2. Matched budgets. Before: "Quality is compared at these matched budgets". After: quality is compared "at matched tuning budgets, not matched spend"; the system's write spend, which no baseline incurs, is charged in the 10.4 ledger. Why: says plainly what is and is not matched.
3. Design effect. Before: "the smallest difference worth acting on". After: "the true difference the test is powered to detect". Why: an observed gain below the design effect can still be significant, so the old wording overstated what the bar guarantees. Raising the bar to require an observed gain at the design effect would halve power, so the bar is unchanged and every gain is now reported with its confidence interval.
4. Implied bar. Before: at least about 0.66 of the design effect at the planned size and 0.47 at the cap, computed at one-sided 0.05. After: about 0.76 and 0.53, computed at the nominal one-sided 0.03 actually used at each look. Why: the figures now match the level the test uses.
5. Preference primary in 10.2. Before: the sizing text referred to "the primary's 0.03 per look" while single-proportion bars were "one look, and no extension", so whether the extension applied was unclear. After: the preference primary "is tested once, at one-sided 0.03, with no extension". Why: removes the ambiguity in the stricter direction; 225 decided pairs, sized at 0.05/3, cover it.
6. Ties and non-inferiority. Before: the non-inferiority exceptions named only 10.5 and the 10.2 fidelity guard. After: they name 10.1 (new item 8) as well. In 10.5, "This is the plan's one non-inferiority test" becomes "It is a non-inferiority test because …", which is wording only; no threshold, sample or rule changed. Why: consistency with item 8.
7. 10.1 secondary metric. Before: "Recall@50 and MRR". After: Recall@50 secondary; MRR "reported as exploratory only, since reciprocal rank is not interval-scaled and is not averaged into any decision". Why: reciprocal rank is not interval-scaled, so its mean should not decide anything.
8. 10.1 per-workspace clause. Before: "no workspace shows a significant degradation", a clause that passes on non-significance in unpowered per-workspace tests. After: in every workspace the lower confidence bound on the Dimension gain at its Holm level lies above −0.03; with about 90 held-out queries per workspace, all three pass with about 80% probability when the true gain is 0.03. Why: a pass now requires positive evidence against a degradation larger than the design effect. Every outcome that failed the old clause fails the new one.
9. 10.1 HyDE escalation. Before: tested on the queries where it fires, with "the firing count and the effect it can detect at 80% power reported before scoring". After: design effect 0.05 nDCG@10, for which about 56 firing queries give 80% power; escalation is kept only if at least that many fire and the difference is positive and significant, and is removed otherwise. Why: the detectable effect is fixed in advance, and an underpowered test cannot keep the escalation.
10. 10.2 baseline (b). Before: "an entity graph with extracted entity claims and community summaries". After: adds that claim extraction is "a step Edge et al. (2024) describe but did not evaluate". Why: says plainly what the baseline relies on.
11. 10.2 small-tier deriver. Before: no arm tested derivation on a small model. After: "An exploratory arm (d) runs the full system with derivation on the small tier", as a check on the argument that derivation needs the strongest model. Why: puts that argument under observation.
12. 10.3 automatic acceptance baseline. Before: 150 automatically accepted Claims judged against an absolute bar only. After: 230 accepted Claims and, as baseline, 230 drawn at random from all Claims the slow path wrote, which is what accepting everything would accept. Annotators are blind to sample. The rule must pass the absolute bar and also exceed the baseline's precision by a one-sided two-proportion test at 0.05, design effect 0.10 (230 per sample give 80% power at 0.8 against 0.7). The samples overlap, which makes the comparison conservative. If the rule fails either test it is disabled. Why: the rule now has to beat accepting everything, not only clear a fixed bar. The sample was raised to power the comparison.
13. 10.4 Graphiti-style comparison. Before: if that arm matched the system's staleness at no higher ingest cost, "the justification network is not earning its complexity on this axis", with no consequence. After: "matches" means the system's probe staleness is not significantly below the arm's, by a one-sided two-proportion test at 0.05 on the pooled final-checkpoint probes (about 110 per arm give 80% power at 0.05 against 0.15). The consequence is binding: the Signal branch of the contradiction check and lifting are removed. Why: the comparison now has a defined test and a binding outcome.
14. 10.4 parity rule. Before: parity is "not significantly better", with no stated rationale. After: the same rule, stated to be deliberate, being "the stricter rule against the system". Why: adding an equivalence margin would let the system set a more expensive read baseline, which would loosen the cost test.
15. 10.4 human curation. Before: Domain creation was "reported as its own line and charged to neither side". After: it is still reported as its own line, and is now charged to the system, with Domain creation listed among the curation actions. The counts include "the Domain creations and revisions in the log window". Why: no curation cost is left out of the break-even figure.
16. 10.4 price-ratio sensitivity. Before: a change in the frontier-to-small price ratio left N*/m "roughly fixed" with the reasoning arm at parity. After: the ratio moves W, g_base and r_base but not H, the ingest line or r_fast, so N*/m is not fixed. A higher ratio lowers it, a lower one can remove break-even, as the illustration shows, and with plain retrieval at parity N*/m rises with the ratio. Why: the old statement contradicted the plan's own illustration.
17. 10.4 illustration. Before: "a fast-path read costs 30 small calls". After: "the equivalent of 30 small calls, mostly cross-encoder scoring with query projection within the 10% bound of Section 10.1". Why: consistency with the 10.1 cost bound. No number changed.
18. 10.4 cost-half test sizing. Before: the bootstrap upper bound on N* had no sample size, power or minimum detectable margin. After: the test is sized before the replay, by simulation from internal-pilot costs and the pilot-log N̄, for 80% power at a minimum detectable margin of a true N* at half of N̄·m. Reads and costed slow-path requests are increased up to three times the planned counts. The extra reads and runs are costed only, never judged, so the quality samples and their cap of twice the planned size are unchanged. The extra runs come from the same log window and also fill the acceptance samples of 10.3. Sizes are reported before scoring, and a shortfall at the cap moves the margin under the shortfall rule. Why: the cost-half test is now powered like every other confirmatory clause. Sample sizes can only rise.
